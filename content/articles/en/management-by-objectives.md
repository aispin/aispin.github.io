## Introduction

Meituan takes Amazon as a learning benchmark in terms of management culture and has been advocating for "Learning from Amazon" since 2022. Therefore, many management concepts are learned and adapted from Amazon, combined with Meituan's own optimization and adjustments. For example, "Leadership Principles," "Growth Flywheel," "Operation Plans (OP)," "Team Goals (TG)," etc. Among them, the methodology related to goal management is mainly "Operation Plans (OP)." Since the second half of 2022, Meituan has vigorously promoted OP and launched a new goal management system in conjunction with "Operation Plans." Although OKR and TG are no longer mentioned in internal Meituan documents, OKR is still used by execution departments to track organizational goals.

There is a significant mixture of Chinese and English in internal Meituan documents, and there is a tendency to use English abbreviations, such as OP, TG, KP (Key Person), etc. For newly hired employees, it may be confusing at first. Fortunately, starting from 2023, Vice President Mu Rongjun has issued a document requiring the replacement of various English abbreviations with Chinese. However, changing habits takes time. In addition, in 2023, Wang Puzhong, the head of the Daojia business group and Vice President of the company, proposed the implementation of "management subtraction," believing that in the past, there was excessive imitation of Amazon's management methodologies, and it is necessary to optimize and simplify them based on Meituan's own situation. This topic can be discussed in another article.

In general, as of the end of 2023, Meituan's goal management consists of Operation Plans (OP), Team Goals (TG), and OKR combined:

1. Major departments (such as the first-level department "Daojia R&D Department" and the second-level department "Shangou Technology Department") primarily use Operation Plans (OP).
2. Subordinate teams and individuals use OKR to break down the Operation Plans into sub-goals.

Of course, there may also be some third and fourth-level departments that use Operation Plans to manage team goals, such as the terminal development group I was in and the terminal group I was responsible for. Both groups practiced Operation Plans in 2023.

It is worth mentioning that Operation Plans are mainly bottom-up, starting from the operation plans of lower-level departments, gradually refining them at each level, and finally selecting the most important goals for the entire company by the core decision-making level (S-Teams), known as S-Team Goals.

## Operation Plans (OP), S-Team Goals & OKR

Operation Plans (OP) are usually annual and involve decisions regarding business indicators and resource allocation. An effective operation plan aligns and reaches consensus between each business group and the core management team (S-Team). It mainly covers the following aspects:

- Financial plan for the next year: including a complete profit and loss statement (P&L) and a detailed plan for employee headcount (HC).
- Key inputs and measures for the next year: identifying the key inputs (such as launching new products, expanding product categories, etc.) and key initiatives required to achieve the financial plan.

Team Goals, Objectives and Key Results (OKR), and KPIs used by some teams within the company are all tools for goal management, but with slightly different focuses.

Based on benchmarking with Amazon's practices, Meituan's unified requirement in 2023 is that business group/platform department-level organizations should use operation plans to manage goals and plans. Teams at lower levels, including individual goal management, can decide which tools to use based on their own circumstances.

Since 23, the Waima Terminal team I am responsible for has actively responded to the company's management policies and has been using operation plans to manage team goals. Subordinate organizations (Waima Terminal Group 1, Group 2) align with the Waima product team and use OKR for goal management.

## Goal Management and Process Review without PPT

Compared to JD and Tencent, the biggest characteristic of Meituan's goal management and process review (including promotion and job performance) is that there is no need to write PPT. Instead, it is based on descriptive documents (STAR principle). The typical process is as follows:

- **Before the meeting**: Spend 10-15 minutes reading the document.
- **During the meeting**: Ask questions and discuss.
- **After the meeting**: Provide feedback and summary (including positive and negative feedback).

Therefore, Meituan places great importance on employees' writing skills. Its document system is very powerful and can be considered the best document system the author has ever seen (comparable to Tencent Docs, a commercial product). In daily work, employees only need to focus on delivering good results in their job, while continuously improving the accumulation and documentation process, enhancing their own writing abilities, and naturally feeling more at ease during performance reviews and promotions.

Note: Meituan's use of descriptive documents instead of PPT for meetings is also inspired by Amazon. However, PPT is still required for presenting achievements and plans during the annual and mid-year business group management meetings.

## OKR Management Requirements

Next, let's talk about some requirements for using OKR for goal management in the subordinate organization during my time at Meituan. Some of the content is borrowed from the product team of Meituan's Waima business and is for reference only.

In addition, for more knowledge about OKR management practices, I recommend the book "OKR Working Method: High-performance Secrets of Top Companies such as Google and LinkedIn". You can apply it according to your own situation.

### Principles

The three key principles are as follows:

1. O (Objectives) represents goals, which need to reflect business/product/technical value, be inspiring, and be executable and controllable. The quantity should be limited to 3-4;
2. KR needs to comply with the SMART principle, which means they should be specific, measurable, challenging, and limited to 3-4;
3. The key to OKR is to align the consensus of peers, superiors, and subordinates on goals and expected results.

Regarding SMART, it is the basic requirement for OKR:

- Specific: Objectives should be clear and specific, without ambiguity.
- Measurable: Objectives and key results should be quantifiable for tracking and evaluation.
- Achievable: Objectives should be realistic and feasible, without being overly idealistic.
- Relevant: Objectives should be relevant to the organization's or individual's strategic direction and needs.
- Time-bound: Objectives should have a deadline for tracking progress.

### 5.4 Common Questions about OKRs

#### Q1: Can OKRs be modified once they are set?

A: Yes, but it requires going through the modification process, explaining the background/reason for the modification, and keeping records of the changes. In principle, we are more willing to accept results caused by uncontrollable external factors or adjustments in business direction.

#### Q2: What is the relationship between OKRs and performance evaluation?

A: The implementation of OKRs plays an important role in performance evaluation, but completion rate does not directly equate to performance. Performance is also influenced by other dimensions. At the same time, the completion of OKRs needs to be evaluated in combination with the complexity of the OKRs.
